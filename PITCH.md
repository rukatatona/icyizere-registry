# Icyizere Registry — the business case

## The problem

Rwandan families who hire a maid — someone who often lives inside their
home, around their children and belongings — have almost no reliable way to
know who they're letting in.

- There is no formal system for checking work history. Hiring runs on word
  of mouth, which can be wrong, incomplete, or dishonest.
- A maid dismissed from one household for theft, mistreatment, or
  unreliability can simply move to the next house, and no one there will
  ever know.
- Existing staffing/placement agencies are paid by commission, which creates
  a conflict of interest: some have an incentive to encourage a maid to
  leave a paying household for one that pays the agency a placement fee,
  regardless of whether that's good for the family or true to what actually
  happened.
- There is no dedicated legal framework protecting domestic workers in
  Rwanda either, so mistreatment can flow in both directions with no honest
  record of what happened at any given house.

Every hire is a guess, and the people best positioned to know the truth —
previous employers — have no structured way to share it.

## The solution

A verification and work-history registry: a neutral, paid service that
tells a family the truth about someone before they let that person into
their home.

Not a staffing agency. No placement, no commission on a hire. We verify,
record, and keep an honest, growing history on each worker — and families
pay directly for that, each time they need it.

## How it works

**1. Initial verification** (when a maid first joins the registry)
- Confirm identity against national ID
- Pull her Criminal Record Clearance Certificate (issued through IremboGov)
- Health certificate, where relevant
- Call her most recent previous employer(s), if any, and record what they say

**2. Ongoing record-keeping** (the core of the business, not a one-time
certificate)

Every time she moves to a new household, someone comes back to the
registry: the new family pays for a lookup on her existing file, plus an
updated reference check with her most recent employer. That placement gets
added to her history — how long she stayed, why it ended, what that family
says about her.

## Who pays, and why

The hiring family pays — never the maid, never a placement commission.

- **First-time verification fee** (higher): identity check, criminal record
  pull, first reference calls.
- **Lookup / update fee** (lower, recurring): each time a family considers
  hiring someone with an existing file, they pay to access her history and
  add one fresh reference check.

## Why this is different

- Not a placement/staffing agency — no stake in whether a hire happens.
- No commission conflict of interest — nothing to gain from encouraging a
  maid to switch households.
- A neutral record-keeper both sides can trust.

## Why it compounds

The registry itself is the asset. Each verified worker and each recorded
placement makes the whole system more useful, and harder for anyone else to
copy from a standing start. Every repeat lookup is repeat revenue from the
same file, not a one-time sale.

## Why IremboGov matters here

IremboGov already issues the Criminal Record Clearance Certificate that
sits at the center of this registry's initial verification step. This
project doesn't compete with that system — it's built on top of it, turning
a document a family would otherwise never see into something they actively
use before hiring. A working relationship here could range from a simple
referral (pointing verified workers to Irembo's own certificate request
flow) to a deeper integration down the line.
